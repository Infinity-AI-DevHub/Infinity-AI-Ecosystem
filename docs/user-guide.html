Chapter 01

Getting started

Installing the application, activating your account, and what to expect the first time you open it.

Installing

Download the installer for your machine from the address your administrator gives you. The application is not code-signed, so both operating systems will warn you the first time. This is expected and happens once per version.

macOS

  1. Open the .dmg and drag Infinity Workspace to Applications.
  2. Open it. macOS says it cannot verify the developer.
  3. Go to System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down, and click Open Anyway.
  4. Open the app again and confirm.

Windows

  1. Run Infinity Workspace Setup.exe.
  2. SmartScreen warns the publisher is unrecognised. Click More info, then Run anyway.
  3. Follow the installer. It installs for you only and needs no administrator rights.

Activating your account

You cannot create your own account. An administrator adds you, and you receive an email with a single-use activation link that expires in 72 hours.

First sign-in

  1. Open the activation link from your email. It opens in your browser, not the app.
  2. Choose a password — at least 12 characters.
  3. Open the desktop application and sign in with your work email and that password.
If the link has expired

Ask an administrator to send a new one. They can do this from People.

How long you stay signed in

The desktop app keeps you signed in for five days from when you sign in. After that it asks for your password again, however much you have been using it. There is no idle timeout — the app relies on your computer's own lock screen.

Signing in on the website (share links, activation, reset) works differently: that session ends after 30 minutes of inactivity.

Forgotten password

Resetting

  1. On the sign-in screen, click Forgot password?
  2. Enter your work email address and submit.
  3. Open the link in the email that arrives. It works once and expires in an hour.
  4. Choose a new password. This signs you out on every device.
The same message either way

The screen says the same thing whether or not the address has an account. That is deliberate — it stops anyone using the page to discover who works here.

Chapter 02

Finding your way around

The parts of the window that stay the same wherever you are.

The sidebar

Every module you have access to. You only see what your role permits — if a module is missing from your sidebar, you do not have access to it, and that is not a fault.

Search

The bar at the top searches across chat messages, files, tasks, meetings, announcements and documents. Press / from anywhere to jump into it. Results only ever include things you are already allowed to see.

Notifications

The bell shows unread notifications. On the desktop these also appear as system notifications, and the number appears on the dock or taskbar icon, so you see them without the window open. Clicking a notification brings the window forward and takes you to whatever it is about.

Managing them

  1. Click the bell to open the panel.
  2. Click any notification to go to what it refers to; it is marked read.
  3. Use Mark all read to clear the badge.

The connection indicator

At the bottom of the sidebar. Live means the app is connected and updates arrive instantly. Reconnecting means it has lost the connection and is retrying — usually a network blip. Nothing is lost while disconnected, but you will not see other people's changes until it reconnects.

Updates

When a new version is released, a bar appears at the top of the window. On Windows the update installs when you restart the app. On macOS you download and install it yourself — an unsigned Mac application is not allowed to replace itself.

Chapter 04

Meetings

Scheduling, responding to and joining meetings. Times are always shown in your own time zone, set in Settings.

Seeing your schedule

The left panel lists the next two weeks, grouped by day. Click any meeting to see its detail. You only see meetings you are invited to.

Scheduling a meeting

Create a meeting

  1. Click Schedule a meeting.
  2. Give it a title, a date and a start time.
  3. Choose a duration and, if you need one, a room.
  4. Tick the colleagues to invite.
  5. Click Schedule meeting.

Everyone invited is notified and the meeting appears in their schedule. You are recorded as accepted automatically, since you organised it.

Responding to an invitation

Open the meeting and choose Accepted, Tentative or Declined. Everyone invited can see who has responded and how.

Joining

Meetings with video show a Join video meeting button. If video has not been configured for the company, the meeting still schedules normally and the join button explains that it is unavailable rather than failing silently.

Changing or cancelling

The organiser can edit the time, room, agenda and attendee list, or cancel it entirely. Everyone invited is notified of either.

Rooms

A room can only be booked by one meeting at a time. If it is taken, you are told when you try to save rather than after the fact.

Chapter 05

Chat

Channels for teams and direct messages between two people.

Channels and direct messages

The left panel lists everything you are a member of. A number beside a room means unread messages. Selecting a room marks it read.

Starting a conversation

A new channel

  1. Click New channel.
  2. Name it and add a topic if useful.
  3. Choose whether it is private (invited members only) or open to the company.
  4. Add the people who should be in it.

A direct message

  1. Click Message someone.
  2. Search for the colleague and select them.

If you already have a conversation with that person, you are taken to it rather than starting a second one.

Sending and editing

  • Type in the box at the bottom and press Enter to send.
  • You can edit or delete your own messages. An edited message is marked as edited.
  • React to a message to acknowledge it without adding to the conversation.
  • Mention a colleague to notify them specifically.
  • Attach a file — it goes through the same storage and virus scanning as Files.
Moderation

Administrators can delete any message. Deletion is recorded in the audit trail, so it is visible that something was removed and by whom.

Chapter 06

Tasks

Work organised into projects and tracked on a board.

Projects

Choose a project from the dropdown at the top. Each project has its own board and its own members. Anyone who can create projects can start one.

Create a project

  1. Click New project.
  2. Give it a name and a short key — the key prefixes every task reference, for example Q3P-14.
  3. Add members.

The board

Five columns: Todo, In Progress, Review, Blocked and Done. Each card shows its reference, its due date, and a coloured dot for priority.

Creating and updating tasks

Add a task

  1. Click New task.
  2. Enter a title, and optionally a description, assignee, priority and due date.
  3. Save.

To move a task, use the status dropdown on its card. To see everything about it, click the title.

Inside a task

  • Comments — discussion attached to the work rather than in chat.
  • History — who changed what, and when.
  • Dependencies — other tasks this one waits on.
  • Watchers — people notified of changes without being assigned.
Two people editing at once

If someone else saves a task while you have it open, your save is refused and you are asked to reload. Nothing you typed is lost, and neither is their change.

Chapter 07

Files

Company file storage, with folders, version history and a recycle bin.

Browsing and uploading

Folders are on the left; the files in the selected folder fill the table.

Upload a file

  1. Select the folder you want it in.
  2. Click Upload and choose the file.
  3. Wait for it to finish — it is scanned before it becomes available.
If an upload is rejected

Files are scanned for malware. A file found to be infected is quarantined and cannot be downloaded by anyone. You are told plainly rather than the upload appearing to fail.

What you can do with a file

ActionWhat happens
DownloadOpens your computer's save dialog so you choose where it goes.
Version historyEvery version ever uploaded, with who uploaded it. Older versions stay downloadable.
Share outside Manager+Creates an expiring link for someone with no account. See chapter 15.
Move to recycle binRemoves it from the folder without destroying it.
RestoreBrings a recycled file back.

New versions

Uploading a file with the same name into the same folder creates a new version rather than a duplicate. The previous version is kept.

Folders

Click New folder to add one. Folders can be nested.

Chapter 08

Documents

The company's written knowledge — policies, runbooks, decision records, onboarding notes. Anything worth writing down and keeping.

Spaces

A space groups related pages. Most should be readable by everyone; restricted spaces exist for the few things that genuinely are not, such as board papers.

Create a space Manager+

  1. Click New space.
  2. Name it and describe what lives there.
  3. Choose Everyone in the company or Only people I grant access to.

Writing a page

Create and publish

  1. Select the space, then click New page.
  2. Give it a title and click Create and edit.
  3. Write in the content box. Basic HTML works: headings, paragraphs, lists, links, tables and code.
  4. Add a note saying what changed — the next reader will thank you.
  5. Click Save as draft to keep it private, or Publish to make it visible and searchable.
Drafts are invisible

A draft does not appear in search. Unpublishing a page removes it from search again.

History

Every save is kept. Click History to see the versions, who wrote each one and why. Restore puts an old version back as a new version — nothing is ever erased.

Two people editing at once

Your work is not lost

If someone saves while you are editing, your save is refused and a message appears. Your text stays in the box. Copy what you need, reload, and reapply it. The system will not merge two people's prose automatically, because doing so silently produces something neither person wrote.

Chapter 09

Announcements

Company-wide notices. One-way — for discussion, use chat.

Reading

Unread announcements are shown in bold. Opening one marks it read. Priority is shown by a coloured bar: normal, important, or critical.

Acknowledging

Some announcements require acknowledgement. Open it and click the acknowledge button. Whoever published it can see who has and has not.

Publishing Manager+

Publish an announcement

  1. Click New announcement.
  2. Write a title and the body.
  3. Set the priority.
  4. Choose the audience: everyone, specific departments, or specific groups.
  5. Tick Requires acknowledgement if people must confirm they have read it.
  6. Publish now, or schedule it for later.

After publishing you can see acknowledgement statistics, and delete an announcement if it was sent in error.

Chapter 10

Approvals

Requests that need someone's decision — expenses, purchases, leave and anything else configured for your company.

The three tabs

TabWhat is in it
Awaiting meRequests where the decision is yours right now.
Raised by meEverything you have submitted, and where it has got to.
All requestsEverything you are permitted to see.

Raising a request

Submit for approval

  1. Click New request.
  2. Choose the type — the form changes to match it.
  3. Fill it in and submit.

Each request gets a reference such as EXPENSE-00014. The route it will take is shown on the request itself, so you can see who it goes to and in what order.

Deciding

Open a request from Awaiting me and choose:

  • Approve — it moves to the next step, or completes.
  • Return for changes — it goes back to whoever raised it.
  • Reject — it ends.

Add a comment explaining your decision. Every decision is recorded permanently.

Why you sometimes see no buttons

Decision buttons only appear if the decision is genuinely yours. You cannot approve your own request, and you cannot approve on someone else's behalf.

Cancelling

Whoever raised a request can cancel it while it is still pending.

If the approver is away

Approvals automatically route to whoever is covering them. See chapter 18 for arranging cover for yourself.

Chapter 11

Leave

Booking time off, and seeing what you have left.

Your balance

The cards at the top show each leave type. The large number is what you have left, and beneath it the breakdown: days taken, days awaiting approval, and your total entitlement. Booked-but-unapproved days are already counted against you, so the number is what you can actually still book.

Booking leave

Book time off

  1. Click Book leave.
  2. Choose the type — annual, sick, unpaid and so on.
  3. Pick the first day and the last day. For a single day, leave the last day blank.
  4. Tick Half day on either end if you only need part of that day.
  5. Add a note if it helps your approver.
  6. Click Book leave.
Weekends and holidays are free

Only working days count. A week off that contains a public holiday costs you four days, not five.

Most types go to your manager for approval and appear in your Approvals list. Some types, such as sick leave, are recorded immediately without approval.

Things that will be refused

  • Dates that overlap leave you have already booked.
  • A range containing no working days.
  • More days than you have left — the message tells you both numbers.

Cancelling

Click Cancel on any pending or approved booking. The days go straight back to your balance.

Who is away Manager+

Managers see a panel listing everyone off in the next four weeks — worth checking before planning anything.

Chapter 12

Finance

Four tabs: expense claims, budgets, suppliers and company equipment. You see only the tabs your role permits.

Expense claims

Make a claim

  1. Click New claim.
  2. Say what it is for, and pick a budget if it belongs to one.
  3. Fill in the first line: date, category, merchant, amount and any tax.
  4. Attach the receipt. Some categories require one above a certain amount.
  5. Click Add line for each further receipt.
  6. Click Save draft.

The running total is shown as you type, but the figure that counts is worked out from the lines when you save.

Submit it

  1. Open the claim from the list.
  2. Check the lines.
  3. Click Submit for approval.

A claim moves through Draft → Submitted → Approved → Reimbursed, or is rejected. Approved and paid are separate on purpose, so an approved claim nobody has paid is visible rather than forgotten.

Recording payment Admin

Open an approved claim, enter the payment reference and mark it paid.

Separation of duties

Whoever approved a claim cannot also record its payment, and nobody can pay their own claim. This is enforced, not a convention.

Budgets Admin

Each card shows what is left, with a bar splitting money already spent from money committed — approved but not yet paid. Committed money is gone for planning purposes even though it has not left the account.

Create a budget

  1. Click New budget.
  2. Name it, set the period and the amount.

Suppliers Manager+

Companies you buy from. Add one with Add supplier. A supplier can be linked to a client organisation so both screens refer to the same company.

Equipment Manager+ to view, Admin to change

The register of laptops, phones, monitors and anything else worth tracking.

Add equipment

  1. Click Add equipment.
  2. Give it an asset tag, a name and a category.
  3. Record the serial number and purchase cost if you have them.

Assign or return it

  1. Click Assign beside the item.
  2. Choose who is taking it, or Nobody — back in stock to return it.
  3. Note its condition.

Every assignment is kept, so "who had this in March" has an answer. Search by tag, name or serial number.

Chapter 13

Growth

Performance reviews and personal goals.

Reviews

Split into About you and Yours to write, so what you owe is obvious.

Write your self-assessment

  1. Open your review from About you.
  2. Write in the self-assessment box.
  3. Click Save self-assessment.

Review someone Manager+

  1. Open the review from Yours to write.
  2. Read their self-assessment — visible to you, but not editable.
  3. Write your assessment and choose an overall rating.
  4. Click Save without sharing to keep working, or Save and share to release it.
Nothing is seen before you share it

The person being reviewed cannot see your assessment until you share it. Until then they are told only that it has not been shared yet.

Goals

Set and track a goal

  1. Type it into the box and click Add.
  2. Drag the slider as you make progress.
  3. Set the status separately: active, at risk, achieved or dropped.

Progress and status are separate because "eighty percent done but at risk" is a real state and the one worth flagging. The percentage turns amber when a goal is at risk.

Review cycles Admin

Open a cycle

  1. Go to the Cycles tab.
  2. Name it, set when it opens and closes.
  3. Click Open cycle.
Check the reporting line first

A review is created for everyone who has a manager set. Anyone without one gets no review at all.

Chapter 14

People

The company directory, and the administrative operations that go with it.

Finding someone

Search by name or email, and filter by status. Select anyone to see their profile. Guests from client companies never appear here — they are in Clients.

Adding someone Admin

Add a colleague

  1. Click Add someone.
  2. Enter their work email — it must be on a verified company domain.
  3. Enter their name, job title, access level and department.
  4. Click Create account.
  5. Send them the activation link that appears. It is single-use and expires in 72 hours.

You never set a password for someone else. If the link expires, issue a new one from their profile.

Changing someone's access Admin

On their profile, change the access level dropdown. Only a super administrator can grant or remove administrator access, and nobody can change their own.

Suspending Admin

Suspending closes every session and live connection immediately. It is reversible — use Reactivate. Files and history are kept. A reason is required and recorded.

Offboarding Admin

For someone leaving for good. This is permanent.

Offboard someone

  1. Open their profile and click Offboard.
  2. Choose who takes over their work.
  3. Give a reason and their last day.
  4. Confirm.

Their projects, open tasks, files, folders, direct reports and any approval waiting on them all move to the successor in one step. Finished tasks stay with them, because reassigning those would misattribute the work.

Equipment does not move by itself

If they are holding company equipment, the dialog lists it. Closing the account does not collect a laptop — arrange the return, then mark each item back into stock in Finance.

Employment records Admin

Their profile shows their employment history: job titles, dates, employment type and the reason for each change. Older records stay visible, dimmed.

Record a change of terms

  1. Click Record change.
  2. Enter the new job title, type and effective date.
  3. Enter the salary if you are permitted to see pay.
  4. Give a reason, such as "Promotion".
Pay is separately restricted

Seeing an employment history does not mean seeing salaries. Where pay is withheld the row says so rather than showing a blank, so it is never mistaken for "nothing recorded". Salaries are encrypted and never written to the audit trail.

A change cannot be back-dated over terms someone was already paid under. That would be a correction rather than an edit, and it should be visible as one.

Chapter 15

Clients

The organisations you work with, and everyone outside the company who can reach your work. Two ways to share: a guest account for an ongoing relationship, a link for a one-off handover.

Organisations

Add an organisation Manager+

  1. Click Add organisation.
  2. Name it and choose the relationship: client, vendor, partner or contractor.
  3. Add a website and notes if useful.

Guest accounts

A guest is a named person at a client who signs in with their own work address. They never appear in the People directory and can reach nothing until you share something specific with them.

Invite a guest Manager+

  1. Select the organisation and click Invite guest.
  2. Enter their work email, name and role.
  3. Set when their access ends. Leave blank for 90 days.
  4. Send them the activation link that appears.

Expand any guest to see exactly what they can reach. Their access end date is shown beside their name, and turns amber as it approaches.

Remove a guest's access

  1. Expand the guest.
  2. Click Revoke all access.

This closes their sessions and expires everything shared with them at once.

A colleague cannot be a guest

An email on your own company domain is refused here. Colleagues are added through People.

Share links

For sending a file to someone who should not need an account at all.

Share a file outside the company Manager+

  1. In Files, click the share icon on the file.
  2. Choose how long the link lasts — 1 to 90 days.
  3. Optionally limit how many times it can be opened.
  4. Optionally set a password, and send it separately from the link.
  5. Record who it is for, then create and copy the link.
A link is a key

Anyone holding the link has the access. Keep them short-lived, and use a password for anything sensitive. You can withdraw a link at any time, and every use is recorded.

  • Overdue approvals — requests past their due date, and who they are with.

Chapter 17

Administration Admin

Five tabs covering the health of the system and the settings behind it.

Operations

Background work — events waiting, the oldest one, and dead letters over the past week. Also account counts. Dead letters mean something failed repeatedly and needs investigation.

Audit trail

Every significant action: who did what, to what, from which address and when. It cannot be edited or deleted by anyone, including administrators — the database itself refuses.

Export it

  1. Filter by date, actor or action.
  2. Click Export for a file you can keep or hand to an auditor.

Groups

Named sets of people, used for announcement audiences and access grants. Create a group, then manage its members.

Company

The company name and legal name, and the verified email domains. Only addresses on a verified domain can be given accounts.

Domains matter more than they look

Removing a domain stops new accounts being created on it. Get this wrong and nobody can be added.

Leave

Three things to configure before anyone books time off:

  • Leave types — whether each is paid, needs approval, and deducts from a balance.
  • Public holidays — the calendar for the year.
  • Entitlement — days per person per type, edited directly in the grid.
Set the holidays before anyone books

A missing public holiday does not fail loudly. It silently charges every employee a day of their own entitlement for a day the office was shut.

Chapter 18

Your settings

Everything about your own account.

Profile

Your display name, job title and time zone. The time zone matters — every meeting time you see is converted into it. Use an identifier such as Asia/Colombo.

Password

Change your password

  1. Enter your current password, then the new one — at least 12 characters.
  2. Click Change password.

Changing it signs you out everywhere, including this device, and you sign in again with the new one. Getting the current password wrong just shows an error — it does not sign you out.

Cover while you are away

Approvals routed to you go to whoever is covering, for as long as the window lasts. It ends by itself.

Arrange cover

  1. Choose the colleague covering for you.
  2. Set the dates.
  3. Leave hand over decisions already waiting on me ticked unless you intend to come back to them.
  4. Click Arrange cover.

Withdraw cover early with Withdraw. Ended cover stays listed, dimmed.

Signed-in devices

Every session on your account, with the device, address and when it was last used. Click Revoke on anything you do not recognise — that session ends immediately.

Chapter 19

For people outside the company

What clients and new joiners see. They use a website, never the desktop app.

Opening a share link

The page shows the file name, its size, and when the link stops working — nothing else about your company. If a password was set, they enter it. Then they download.

Expired, withdrawn, used-up and never-existed links all show the same message. That is deliberate: telling them apart would let someone confirm a guessed link.

Activating an account

Both colleagues and guests activate the same way — open the emailed link, choose a password. Guests then sign in on the website; colleagues install the desktop app.

Resetting a password

Anyone with an account can reset from the website, whether or not they can get into the app.

Chapter 20

Search

One search across everything you are allowed to see.

Press / anywhere, or click the search bar. Results are grouped by type — chat, files, tasks, meetings, announcements and documents — and you can filter to one type. Matching words are highlighted in the snippet.

You cannot search your way into something

Search results respect the same permissions as everything else. A private channel you are not in, or a restricted document space, will never appear.

Chapter 21

When something goes wrong

The app says it cannot connect

Check your internet connection. The indicator at the bottom of the sidebar shows whether the app is reaching the server. If it stays on Reconnecting for more than a minute or two, tell your administrator.

You were signed out unexpectedly

Usually one of:

  • Five days have passed since you signed in.
  • You changed your password — that signs out every device.
  • An administrator suspended the account.
  • Someone revoked the session from Settings.

A button you expect is missing

Almost always permissions. Actions only appear when they are genuinely available to you — the system does not show buttons that would fail. Ask an administrator if you think your access is wrong.

You did not receive an email

Check spam first. Activation links expire after 72 hours and password reset links after an hour. Ask for a new one rather than waiting.

Everyone is locked out

There is a recovery path that needs access to the server itself. Your administrator has the procedure in the deployment guide. It records itself in the audit trail, so a recovery is always visible afterwards.

Chapter 22

Who can do what

Six access levels. Each includes everything below it unless stated otherwise.

LevelIntended for
StaffEveryone. Chat, tasks, files, documents, meetings, own leave and expenses, own goals.
ManagerTeam leads. Approve requests, see who is away, publish announcements, manage clients and guests, share files externally, conduct reviews, view reports and equipment.
AuditorRead-only oversight. The audit trail, employment histories and reports — but never salaries.
AdminRuns the company day to day. Accounts, offboarding, employment records including pay, budgets, reimbursement, leave configuration, review cycles, groups, company settings.
Super adminEverything an admin can do, plus granting and removing administrator access.
GuestExternal. Only what has been explicitly shared, and only until their access expires.
Two rules that apply to everyone

Nobody can approve their own request, and whoever approves an expense cannot also record its payment. These hold regardless of access level, including for super administrators.
